BEFORE THE APPELLATE AUTHORITY

SECURITIES AND EXCHANGE BOARD OF INDIA 
Appeal No. 1037 of 2010 
Madan Kumar Chaudhary       :               Appellant 
Vs. 
CPIO, SEBI, Mumbai        :                   Respondent 
                  
         ORDER 
 
1.   The appellant had filed an application dated September 21, 2010 under the Right to 
Information Act, 2005 (RTI Act). The respondent, vide letter dated November 18, 2010, 
responded to the appellant.  Aggrieved by the said response, the appellant has filed this 
appeal dated December 01, 2010 (received on December 06, 2010). 
   
2. I have carefully examined the application, the response and the appeal and find that 
the matter can be decided on merit based on the material available on record. 
 
3. I find that the appellant had not sought fo r any particular document or record under 
these items. Nor did he ask for information ava ilable with the public authority. For example: 
“1. If a customer is cheated by a tr ading member (i.e KBSL) by way of ma king client’s forged signature on 
the Ledger Balance / other Documents and showing client’s flimsy presence at trading member’s office while 
client is physically present full time in his office at some other place in other State on that day. What action 
can a client take against the Trading Member?”   Like this, the appellant had asked questions, 
explanations, clarifications, opinions etc. in th e guise of seeking information. Under the RTI 
Act, the obligation of the respondent is to  provide the information which should be 
identifiable and available in the records of th e public authority, as held by the Hon’ble CIC 
in the matters of Shri M. M. Sundram Vs. SEBI 1, Shri Aakash Aggarwal Vs. Shri Karam 
Chand & Anr.2 , Shri Vibhor Dileep Barla Vs. Central Excise & Customs 3  and S.K. Kapoor 
Vs. CPIO, SEBI & Anr. 4  Nevertheless, the respondent informed the grievance redressal 
mechanism of SEBI as against item 5 though it was a question.  The appellant has not

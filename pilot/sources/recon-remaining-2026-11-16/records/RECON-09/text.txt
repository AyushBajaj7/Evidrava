6. I note that SEBI had conducted invest igation into the buying, selling and 
dealing in the scrip of DASL for the period of August, 2001 and January, 
2002, pursuant to a preferential allotment made by it to another company 
namely Bonanza Biotech Ltd. (hereinafter referred to as ‘BBL’) on swap basis 
in the ratio of 1:1. Duri ng the period of investigation the scrip of DASL was 
listed at the Bombay St ock Exchange (hereinafter referred to as ‘BSE’), 
Madhya Pradesh Stock Exc hange (hereinafter referred to as ‘MPSE’) and 
Ahmedabad Stock Exchange (hereinafter re ferred to as ‘ASE’). Investigation 
report states that DASL had made a preferential allotment of 10 crore shares 
(hereinafter referred to as ‘said shares’) to BBL on October 29, 2001, which 
was about 14 times of the pre-issue paid up capital of DASL. The 
consideration for this was another preferential allotment of 10 crore shares 
made by BBL to DASL on the same date which was about 5 times of the pre-
issue paid up capital of BBL before allotment. No exchange of cash was 
found between the two companies. Pursuant to this, DASL first made an 
application for listing of the said shares allotted to BBL to MPSE and later on 
to BSE also. MPSE granted listing permission albeit with undue haste and 
without exercising due diligence. However, no trading permission was 
granted in respect of the said shares. The listing application which was made 
subsequently to BSE was kept pending, upon apprehension of commission of 
certain violations in the process by DASL. No application for listing was made 
to ASE by DASL. On the basis of li sting permission given by MPSE, the 
Central Depositories Services Ltd. (hereinafter referred to as ‘CDSL’), a 
registered depository, dematerialized the said shares of DASL and credited 
them to the beneficial owner account of BBL. 
 
After allotment, a major chunk of shares allotted by DASL to BBL were 
offloaded by BBL through a network of entities including the noticee, 
Coverage and Consultants, Pinnacle Finstock Pvt. Ltd., C.D. Equisearch, Mr. 
Shyam Parwaney and Mr. Manoj Ganeriw ala on the trading platform of BSE, 
even though these shares were not  listed on BSE. Such offloading was 
contrary to the provisions of BSE bye-laws and constituted bad delivery on

12. I have carefully considered the Enquiry Report, SCN, reply of the noticee, 
written submissions dated August 18, 2009 and July 30, 2010 and the 
material available on record. My findings thereof are as under: 
 
a. I note that the noticee had mainly transacted in the scrip of DASL on behalf of 
two of its clients namely, Murali Nair and VFPL. It has been submitted by the 
noticee that the said clients were trading through it since 1997 and 1999 
(VFPL and Murali Nair, respectively) and complied with their obligations 
regularly and on time. It has also been stated that they traded in fairly large 
quantities in blue chip scrips and other shares. During the Enquiry 
proceedings noticee had submitted the copies of know-your-client (hereinafter 
referred to as ‘KYC’) agreement form of VFPL and Murali Nair. From the

argument of noticee as t he SEBI circular bearing no. SMD/Policy/Circular/5 -

as May 31, 1997.

Murali Nair had transacted only in two scrips namely, DASL and Reliable 
Plastics Ltd. It would be pertinent to note that BBL was originally incorporated 
as Reliable Plastics Ltd. on June 04, 1981. The company changed its name

18. While concluding the matter, I summarize my findings as under:  
 
- Noticee received large number of shares as argued for future trades and 
margin purposes. However, from the records available it is seen that noticee 
has not shown any trade for which said shares could have been given in 
margin. Considering this, I note that noticee should not have accepted these 
shares which were lying idle with it for a considerable period of time. 
 
- Receipt of large number of shares by the director of noticee namely Manoj 
Ganeriwala in off-market from BBL and hi s close relationship with one of the 
director of BBL straight give away of  complicity. He was surely aware about 
preferential allotment of 14 times of the pre-issue capital of the company. A 
director of a company binds the company by his actions, it is immaterial 
whether he was paid a salary or not. Even if, Manoj Ganeriwala would have 
been just an employee, then also the company could not have been absolved 
of all the responsibility. Thus to sum up, I am of the considered opinion that 
the actions of Manoj Ganeriwala as director of noticee binds the noticee in no 
uncertain manner.
